How to actually use a digest when it arrives in your inbox. What to scan for, what to act on, what to skip.

Setting up a digest is the easy part. Reading it well — turning the email into actual coaching, decisions, or actions — is where the real value lives. This page is the guide to that.

The advice here is generic across digest types. For the specifics of what each type contains, see the dedicated type pages.

## The 90-second pass[](https://learn.lumopath.ai/digests/reading-a-digest#the-90-second-pass)

A digest is designed to be skimmed first, then dug into. Here's the pattern most managers settle on after a few weeks:

### Step 1 — Scan top to bottom[](https://learn.lumopath.ai/digests/reading-a-digest#step-1-scan-top-to-bottom)

Open the digest and scroll through the whole thing once before you stop on anything. You're looking for:

- **Anything flagged as urgent** (capacity spikes, accounts going cold, escalations) — these are the things you want to act on before your day starts
    
- **Anything that surprises you** — a person at 130% capacity you didn't expect, an account you thought was healthy showing decline
    
- **Anything you can recognize** — wins, improvements, strong performance worth calling out
    

The goal of the first pass isn't to read carefully — it's to know what's _in_ the digest before you decide what to act on.

### Step 2 — Pick 2–3 things to act on[](https://learn.lumopath.ai/digests/reading-a-digest#step-2-pick-2-3-things-to-act-on)

A typical digest contains more signals than you can act on in a single morning. Don't try to address everything. Pick the 2 or 3 highest-leverage things and ignore the rest.

Good candidates:

- A capacity issue that's been trending wrong for multiple weeks
    
- An account that's gone cold and is approaching renewal
    
- A coaching opportunity tied to a goal someone is currently working on
    
- Something to recognize in your next 1:1
    

Drop the rest. The next digest will surface them again if they're still relevant.

### Step 3 — Take the action[](https://learn.lumopath.ai/digests/reading-a-digest#step-3-take-the-action)

For each thing you picked, do the action _now_ if it's a small one (send a Slack, add an agenda item, schedule a check-in) or block 15 minutes on your calendar if it requires more thought.

The goal is to never have to re-read the digest. Process it once, decide, act or schedule, move on.

## What to scan for, by section type[](https://learn.lumopath.ai/digests/reading-a-digest#what-to-scan-for-by-section-type)

Different parts of a digest are useful for different things. The most common section types you'll see:

### Per-person sections (Weekly Coaching Plan)[](https://learn.lumopath.ai/digests/reading-a-digest#per-person-sections-weekly-coaching-plan)

For each team member, scan for:

- **The "next step" line** — most digests include a suggested action for that person's next 1:1. Read this first.
    
- **Capacity trend** — is it stable, rising, or falling? Multi-week patterns matter more than single-week spikes.
    
- **What changed this week** — wins, escalations, account movement
    
- **What to recognize** — don't skip these, recognition matters as much as coaching
    

Use the per-person sections as the agenda for your 1:1 block. You don't need to copy them — the digest is the prep.

### Account or customer sections[](https://learn.lumopath.ai/digests/reading-a-digest#account-or-customer-sections)

For account-level signals, scan for:

- **Accounts ranked by urgency** — usually at the top of the section
    
- **Engagement drops** — declining communication is often the earliest churn signal
    
- **Contact gaps** — accounts your team hasn't touched in 14+ days
    
- **High-ARR accounts in the at-risk list** — a $300K renewal going cold is a different problem from a $30K one
    

Use these to decide which accounts need an outreach today.

### Team or org-level sections (Executive Digest)[](https://learn.lumopath.ai/digests/reading-a-digest#team-or-org-level-sections-executive-digest)

For aggregated views, scan for:

- **The headline number and trend** — capacity %, KPI rollups, vs. prior period
    
- **Outlier teams or squads** — which one is most concerning, which is healthiest
    
- **Anything labeled systemic** — patterns affecting more than one team
    
- **The narrative summary** — usually written to be quotable in a leadership update
    

These are the parts you can paste into a Slack to your VP, an email to leadership, or a board slide.

## What to skip[](https://learn.lumopath.ai/digests/reading-a-digest#what-to-skip)

A digest will always contain some signals that aren't worth acting on. It's important to be willing to skip them, otherwise the digest becomes overwhelming.

Things that are usually safe to skip:

- **One-week spikes that aren't part of a pattern** — a capacity bump from a vacation week isn't a coaching moment
    
- **Recognition that doesn't match what you know** — the AI is good but not perfect; if it suggests recognizing something that doesn't ring true, trust your context
    
- **Account flags on accounts you already know about** — if you're already on top of the Acme escalation, you don't need the digest to remind you tomorrow
    
- **Anything that's a duplicate of last week** — if the same signal has appeared three weeks running and you've decided not to act, that's still a valid decision
    

The point of a digest is to surface what you might miss, not to give you a homework list.

## When the digest is wrong[](https://learn.lumopath.ai/digests/reading-a-digest#when-the-digest-is-wrong)

If a digest contains something that's clearly incorrect — a person flagged for the wrong reason, an account with wrong data, a recommendation that doesn't match reality — work through this:

1. **Check the data scope.** Is the digest pulling from the right teams, time range, and KPIs? Edit it and verify.
    
2. **Check the underlying integration.** If the data is wrong because Lumopath isn't seeing some communication channel, the fix is at the integration level. See [Data Quality Gotchas](https://learn.lumopath.ai/how-lumopath-works/data-quality-gotchas).
    
3. **Send yourself a fresh test** to confirm the issue is reproducible.
    
4. **Flag it to** [**support@lumopath.ai**](mailto:support@lumopath.ai) with the digest name, the specific section that was wrong, and what you expected.
    

## Sharing what you read[](https://learn.lumopath.ai/digests/reading-a-digest#sharing-what-you-read)

A digest is yours by default — you decide whether to share. Patterns that work well:

- **Add your boss as a recipient** so you don't have to forward
    
- **Quote specific sections in 1:1s** ("the digest flagged that your contact gap on Acme is now 18 days — what's going on there?")
    
- **Reference it in leadership updates** — "per the weekly coaching digest, the team's capacity is back under 100% after the Q1 spike"
    

What _not_ to do: don't forward a per-person coaching digest to the person it's about. The Coach generates frank observations for the manager to interpret — it's coaching prep, not a performance review.
